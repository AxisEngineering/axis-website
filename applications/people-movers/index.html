Size for the service pattern

Linear-motor thrust acts across an air gap rather than through wheel grip. The route still determines the required force: gradients, loaded mass, acceleration and resistance must all be included.

We assess the complete cycle—pull-away, cruise, braking, station dwell and repetition. Your drive and controls shape the acceleration and approach to each stop within the motor’s electrical and thermal limits.

Route length, fleet size, stop spacing and maintenance access determine the choice; neither arrangement is automatically better.

Braking and holding

A suitable motor and drive can provide electromagnetic braking. A permanent-magnet eddy-current brake adds passive dynamic braking without friction pads or an electrical supply.

A separate holding brake is needed wherever the vehicle must remain stationary. Eddy-current braking falls to zero at standstill. Low-speed stopping, platform holding and gradient safety belong to the wider system design.

Motor supply, not a complete transit system

Axis sizes, simulates and supplies the motor and optional magnetic brake. Vehicle, track, controls, holding brake and operation of the service remain with you or your integrator.

  • Share empty and loaded mass, speeds, stop spacing and gradients.
  • Include dwell times, service frequency, supply limits and the running gap.
