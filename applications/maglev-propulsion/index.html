Propulsion is separate from levitation

The linear motor supplies longitudinal thrust; suspension and guidance support and steer the vehicle. Axis’s scope here is the propulsion motor, not the complete levitation system.

A travelling magnetic field acts on a conductive reaction plate across an air gap. A variable-frequency drive changes field speed as the vehicle accelerates, while current, slip and thermal limits determine usable thrust.

Match the motor gap to the suspension

Reaction-rail material and geometry affect thrust. The suspension must maintain the gap assumed in the motor design across speed, load and alignment variations.

Propulsion and suspension teams therefore need one agreed gap budget. A nominal-gap result alone does not establish performance over the vehicle’s full movement envelope.

Assess the complete journey

  • Route: speed profile, gradients, acceleration and station stops.
  • Electrical: drive frequency, voltage, current and sectional energisation.
  • Thermal: motor and reaction-rail temperatures over repeated service.
  • Interfaces: gap tolerance, mounting loads and guidance requirements.

Axis records the layout assumptions and predicted operating range so the propulsion interfaces can be checked with the wider system.
