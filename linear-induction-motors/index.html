How a LIM works

A powered primary creates a travelling magnetic field. This induces currents in a conductive reaction plate; their interaction produces thrust across an air gap. Think of a rotary induction motor unrolled into a straight line.

During motoring, the field moves faster than the plate. This speed difference—slip—affects thrust, current and plate heating. Gap, plate material and operating speed also matter.

Thrust does not depend on wheel–rail grip. Gradients still add load, and the running gap and environmental conditions remain design inputs.

Single- or double-sided?

Motor configurations
Design choiceSingle-sided (SSLIM)Double-sided (DSLIM)
ArrangementOne primary faces a conductive plate, usually backed by steel.Two primaries face a bare aluminium or copper fin.
Mechanical loadsThe structure and bearings must carry the normal magnetic pull.Symmetry can balance normal forces on the fin; the frame still carries magnetic loads.
ChoiceOne active face and no second-primary frame.Two active faces can provide more thrust within a given track width; centring and support matter.

Plate choice and sizing

Aluminium is a common starting point where moving mass matters; copper is another option. Higher conductivity is not automatically better: plate material and thickness must suit the slip and speed range.

  • Duty: mass, thrust, acceleration, target speed, route, gradients, stops and cycle time.
  • Installation: available space, mounting, running gap and any existing plate or rail.
  • Limits: supply, temperature, weight and cost.
