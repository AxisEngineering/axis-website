Selection guide

Single-sided or double-sided LIM?

Start with access to the reaction plate and the loads your structure can carry. Then compare both layouts against the same speed, supply and duty.

One active face or two?

The primary is the powered motor winding. The reaction plate is the conductor on which it acts. These sketches show sections across the motor, with travel into or out of the page.

In a symmetric double-sided layout, normal forces on a centred fin can balance. The motor frame still carries magnetic loads, and offset or misalignment can produce a net force on the fin.

Your starting point

  • Only one plate face is accessible: start by assessing a single-sided motor and the structure needed to carry its attraction load.
  • Both faces are accessible and a bare fin suits the vehicle: assess a double-sided layout, including its second primary, supporting frame and guidance.
  • A plate, rail or mounting is already fixed: check its material, thickness, backing and movement envelope before choosing either arrangement.

These are starting points, not a performance ranking. Neither layout is universally more efficient, cheaper or better for high speed.

Compare at the same required thrust–speed duty, supply constraints, available envelope and temperature limits. Count the complete installation: motors, reaction rail, support, guidance and drive. Two active faces do not guarantee twice the usable thrust.

What to check next

Send the moving mass, entry and target speeds, travel distance, gradient, cycle time and available supply. Add a section through the proposed mounting, showing the plate, backing, covers, nominal gaps and expected movement.
